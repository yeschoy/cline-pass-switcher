# Decision memo — high-concurrency request path (2026-09-28)

## What is established, and what is not

**Measured synthetic facts:** default-off sanitized full capture on repeated 1–6 MiB fake bodies added substantial event-loop/CPU work; a 6 MiB/2-in-flight case produced capture-budget omissions and ~0.57 s loop stalls. At 1,200 cloned minutes and ten accounts, a new `load-health` request recomputed health 11 times (528 projections/48 requests) and ranking took ~12 ms/48 requests, while synchronous final metadata `record()` took ~179–184 ms/48; roundrobin on the same corpus took ~175–207 ms in `record()`. Normal fast sparse routing showed small differences inside run-to-run noise. With two accounts and a 40 ms mock, waiting up to 80 ms converted some immediate local capacity 429s into successes but added ~80 ms median latency and did **not** increase the mock pool's intrinsic capacity. All 48 deliberate upstream 429s were real mock attempts, unlike local RPM/capacity 429s. Short and paused SSE showed no compelling A/B advantage; paused ~4 MiB streams generated mock upstream backpressure and included 120 ms intentional downstream delay. Detail byte limits/omissions and per-account leasing worked as designed under these fixtures.

**Not established:** production mix, load, p95/p99/SLO, account/Provider cardinality and rate of dense history, sanitizer inputs, production disk/TLS/network behavior, sustained open socket count, true downstream backpressure rate, rate of opt-in detailed capture or browser render costs. A and B differ in integrated server behavior (not just load-health); two/three fresh-process samples cannot support small percent improvement claims. Archived whole-service/browser/statistics findings use different corpus definitions and cannot be used as numerically matched controls. There is no evidence to deploy or rewrite architecture on the strength of these synthetic tests.

## Ranked conditional opportunities

| Priority / trigger | Evidence and potential benefit | Cost / compatibility counterexample | Recommendation and next proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Opt-in sanitized full diagnostic CPU/loop risk when large bodies are common** | 1 MiB full total CPU ~503–538 ms/12 vs off ~40–49 ms/12, loop max ~35–39 vs ~6 ms; at 6 MiB full, ~1.7–1.9 s total CPU/6, loop max ~0.53–0.58 s and four capture-budget drops/6. The archived isolated sanitizer and five near-limit chats independently flag this path. | **Default is off**. Raw-full is faster in this artificial case *because it intentionally bypasses sanitization*: raw memory ~398–470 MiB vs off ~250–313, and publishing unredacted credentials/content is an unacceptable performance workaround. Bypassing redactor, limit, omission or retention breaks security. Worker isolation or chunking that preserves cross-body secret learning, exact omission reasons, bounded concurrency and fail-open traffic would be a medium/high-risk separate design, not a simple offload. | Keep full only for controlled incident windows; use existing bounded error-only mode where sufficient. First establish sanitized-full traffic/body-size share, CPU profiles with diverse **synthetic** redactable secrets and concurrent 5 MiB bodies, max loop stalls and drop reasons; consider **single-owner** bounded redaction scheduling only after evidence and security review. Do not expose raw capture as an optimization, lower safeguards or add a second capture pipeline. |
| **2. Common completion path: dense-history metadata synchronous save (conditional on real corpus size)** | In the intentionally dense 1,200-minute case, 48/48 completions invoked `record`; stringify ~137–145 ms total, synchronous write ~29–46 ms, rename ~5–18 ms. ~3–4 ms per terminal save can dominate more frequent requests versus new selection (~0.25 ms/selected request); sparse RR1 `record` only ~21–24 ms/96. No unsanctioned scheduler or delayed-persistence refactor. |
| **3. Dense multi-account load-health projection reuse if the mode is actually selected frequently** | B load-health made 528 health projections/48 requests with 1,200 synthetic minute buckets, `strategyRank` ~12.2–12.5 ms, lease ~12.6–12.9 ms; same-corpus RR rank ~0.03 ms, lease ~0.44–0.46 ms. Sparse 10-account `strategyRank` ~0.7 ms/96 and end-to-end RPS overlaps RR variation. | Caching across requests risks stale health/active counts/coverage and changing eligibility. Unknown success rate is deliberately *not zero*, and health/pipeline ordering/short-circuiting are compatibility properties. A cross-request cache or second candidate owner is unjustified. | No immediate code change. If real evidence shows **high account count × dense health history × substantial `load-health` rate**, profile account scans and consider only **request-local** reuse of projections within the existing selection owner, preserving the exact unknown/coverage and lease decisions. Validate against pool mode, sticky restrictions, health/ban/cooldown and quota state. |
| **4. Local overload policies (`maxConcurrent`, RPM, `poolFullWaitMs`)** | 2 × 1 permit, 12-wide burst: 0 wait => 4 successes/24, 20 local 429; 80 ms wait => 8–9 successes, 15–16 local 429, median ~85–87 ms (including deliberate waiting). New pool-specific 80 ms option behaves like inherited 80 ms in this all-concurrency-blocked fixture. | Reduced 429 count is not increased upstream capacity. Waits consume client deadline/connection resources; indiscriminately raising waits worsens latency and may violate expected 429 behavior. Upstream 429 is a different owner; Provider failure cannot silently lease another account. | Tune **existing** waits only per accepted operator latency budget; maintain `blockedBy` and local-vs-upstream distinction. Do not invent a replacement scheduler, replay after visible SSE or add cross-account retries. Test mixed RPM+capacity and timeouts before suggesting policy defaults. |
| **5. Large input / SSE and management reads** | 6 MiB off: `readBody` ~33–41 ms/6 and `injectPrefs` ~18–19 ms/6 in one environment; paused ~4 MiB SSE mock exhibited `write(false)`/`drain` 40–48 times/12 requests. Archived 09-25 statistics projection and dense browser/model DOM have *separate* conditional costs. | Large body, socket buffering and deliberate pause dominate; removing per-Provider body isolation, native backpressure or cancellation/finalization to save copies can break correctness. Admin GET/DOM is not per-chat routing. | No change on current evidence. Re-test realistic large JSON, proxies/TLS, cancellations, slow clients and sustained mixed streams; for UI/statistics follow archived findings only after comparable corpus and UI telemetry. |

Ranking is **impact if the triggering workload exists**, not a universal order for production. Default-off mode #1 cannot be the general service bottleneck unless operators enable it frequently; dense metadata #2 may dominate the *default* path at large histories. Small no-diagnostic sparse traffic showed no clear new feature regression. No runtime optimization is included in this research task.

## Minimal privacy-safe production evidence to decide later (proposal only)

For a bounded time window under existing authenticated admin observability, aggregate **counts/histograms** (no request IDs, headers/values, session identities, body content, keys, proxy URLs, account notes or raw Provider errors):

1. Route mode, enabled eligible-account count bins (1, 2, 3–10, >10), in-flight per-account count **as histogram only**, lease-wait bins, local admission `blockedBy` (`concurrency`, `rpm`, unavailable) versus upstream HTTP status family and true attempt-count bins. Avoid per-account raw identifiers in a general log. Preserve account-before-Provider attribution and distinguish client cancellation from health failure.
2. Request and response byte-size bins, stream flag, active diagnostic profile (`off`/`error`/`full`/`raw`), bounded capture drop reason counts and captured/observed byte bins; metadata minute-bucket count/serialized size bins, terminal save-duration bins, process CPU and event-loop-delay coarse histograms. Never include diagnostic text or sanitized fragments in ordinary logs.
3. Per-mode completion rate and p50/p95/p99 with **window length, counts, sampling fraction, successful/error/cancelled breakdown** and simultaneous in-flight/socket counts. Correlate aggregate upstream wait/TTFB versus local queue/finalization; ordinary request durations alone cannot separate them. Include admin statistics query rate separately from model requests.

Obtain explicit operator permission for telemetry or a controlled export, retention and access policy first. Compare measured mode/profile share against triggers above before opening a **separate** scoped optimization proposal. Performance must preserve stable account lease then per-account Provider chain, same-directory atomic operator writes, bounded ordinary logs, optional sanitized detail limits, authorized raw readiness and streaming/cancellation semantics. Do not deploy based on this memo.
